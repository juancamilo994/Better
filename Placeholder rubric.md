 Proposed eight-axis rubric
Criteria have one home to avoid double-counting. Other axes may link the same evidence without adding another penalty.

Hierarchy and wayfinding — HIER: focal point, primary versus secondary actions, grouping, reading order, navigation labels and location cues. Judge attention and orientation, not raw alignment.The most important element and the primary action stand out immediately. Secondary actions are visually quieter. Related items are grouped, the reading order is clear, and the user can always tell where they are.
Layout and spatial rhythm — LAY: grid, alignment, spacing, proximity, density, whitespace, content width, clipping and overlap. Judge arrangement, not aesthetic fashion. Elements align to a consistent grid with even, intentional spacing. Related items sit close together. Nothing is cramped, clipped, or overlapping, and text blocks stay a comfortable width. Judge arrangement, not trendiness.
Typography and content clarity — TYPE: type scale, size, line length/height, legibility, descriptive headings, labels, instructions, useful content and eliminate microcopy. Separate presentation from message clarity in subcriteria. Text sizes follow a clear scale, and line length and spacing support easy reading. Headings, labels, buttons, and instructions use plain, specific wording. Readability and message clarity are scored separately.
Color and accessibility — ACC: measured contrast where available, non-color cues, visible focus, labels, semantics and target sizing where evidenced. Screenshot review can flag concerns; it cannot certify WCAG compliance or keyboard accessibility. Text and UI elements meet contrast minimums, measured when possible. Meaning never depends on color alone. Focus states are visible, controls are labeled, and tap targets are large enough. Screenshots can flag issues but cannot certify compliance.
Visual craft and consistency — CRAFT: coherent components, palette, typography, borders, radii, elevation, icons, imagery/crops and visual finish. Evaluate intentional execution; style preference belongs in the taste channel. Components, colors, type, borders, corners, shadows, icons, and images follow one consistent system. Details look deliberate and finished. Personal style preference is scored separately.
Interaction and feedback — INT: apparent affordances, hover/focus/selected/disabled states, loading, validation, success/failure, recovery and observed outcomes. Motion requires recordings or temporal evidence. Looks clickable and works when clicked are separate claims. Clickable elements look clickable. Hover, focus, selected, disabled, loading, error, and success states exist and are clear, and users can recover from errors. Appearance and actual behavior are scored separately, and animation is judged only from video.
Adaptation and cross-device quality — ADAPT: reflow, prioritization, navigation changes, orientation, touch/pointer suitability and resilience. Score within-viewport defects in their home criteria; assess adaptation here through matched captures. The layout reflows and reprioritizes content sensibly across screen sizes and orientations. Navigation adapts, and controls suit touch or mouse. Score only from side-by-side captures; bugs within a single screen size belong in their own category.
Purpose, brand and trust — FIT: task fit, audience appropriateness, brand coherence, clarity of claims/pricing/conditions and relevant credibility cues. This evaluates presentation, not whether the company is trustworthy. The design fits its task, audience, and brand. Prices, claims, and conditions are clear and upfront, and credibility cues are present where relevant. Judge presentation, not the company’s actual trustworthiness.

Proposed anchors: 1 = material obstacle to the stated task; 2 = usable with significant defects; 3 = solid with minor defects; 4 = strong and intentional in assessed evidence. Each criterion must add observable examples and evidence requirements. “Beautiful” alone is not an anchor. Avoid decimal scores implying precision unsupported by an ordinal scale.
